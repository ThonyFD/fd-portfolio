The parts of this sheet were written separately and will disagree in small ways. Settle every disagreement in this order:
**house rules** (including type no smaller than 13px and body text of 16px or more) → **the direction** (the idea, the composition habits, where the

## 1. The idea
The first screen is made only of words, colour and space, good enough that nobody misses a picture there. Pictures arrive later, large and few, as relief from the type. This is the honest answer when a subject has nothing worth photographing: instead of inventing illustrations, set what you have to say as well as it can be set. The typography carries a point of view through scale (one enormous statement, everything else small), through measure (a narrow, bookish column or a full-width line), and through one committed colour.

Before anything else, write one sentence in your notes: "On this page, <the subject's own material> becomes <the device>."
Fill the first blank from your inventory of what only this client has (its name and what it means, its street, its numbers, its
constraint), then put the nearest competitor in the sentence: if it still works, the idea is the category's, not the client's.
Then name the one live thing the visitor can do on the page (SKILL.md step 4).

## 2. Composition
First screen: the statement, two lines at most, set at 9–14vw and occupying the full width with tight leading, positioned in the upper two-thirds. One supporting sentence at 20–24px sits bottom-left on the same margin and the single action sits bottom-right on the same baseline. No navigation bar; two or three links in a top corner. Sections that follow are typographic set pieces, each different: a list of five to eight capabilities set at 4–6vw one per line; a paragraph at 28–36px in a 28ch column pushed to the right half; a pair of large numerals with a sentence each; a single quotation filling a colour field. Each section uses a different horizontal position (left, right, full) so the page has movement without imagery.

## 3. Type — Sentient
A soft, low-contrast serif in a light weight, more book than fashion magazine, large and calm in sentence case. Suits outdoor and craft products, restaurants, cameras and slow consumer goods.
```html
<link rel="preconnect" href="https://api.fontshare.com">
<link rel="preconnect" href="https://cdn.fontshare.com" crossorigin>
<link href="https://api.fontshare.com/v2/css?f[]=sentient@300,400,401,500&display=swap" rel="stylesheet">
```
- Display: **Sentient** — weight 300 · tracking -0.015em · leading 1.0 · sentence case; about 8vw for the headline, 40px for statements
- Text: **Sentient** — 400 for body, 500 for emphasis · leading 1.55 · tracking 0
```css
:root {
  --step-small: clamp(0.8125rem, 0.79rem + 0.1vw, 0.875rem);
  --step-body: clamp(1.125rem, 1.05rem + 0.3vw, 1.3125rem);
  --step-lead: clamp(1.25rem, 1.1rem + 0.7vw, 1.625rem);
  --step-head: clamp(2rem, 1.3rem + 3.2vw, 4rem);
}
```
This direction lives or dies on the face and its settings. Display at tight tracking and leading; text sizes few and far apart (for example 15, 22, 34 and the display size). Use real typographic detail: proper quotes, balanced line breaks, hung punctuation where it helps.
The direction decides sizes and how many of them; the type system decides how this face is set. Where the two give different tracking or leading, use the values of the type system: they are tuned to this face.
The display size itself comes from the hero blueprint below.

## 6. Hero — Name across the middle
Paper and ink only; no picture on the first screen. The name runs on one line across the vertical centre, sized to span from the 18px left margin to the 18px right margin (97.5% of the width). Set it as inline SVG text with a `viewBox` fitted to the word and `width: 100%`, or tune its vw size to the letter count, so it never wraps. In a heavy grotesk the letters stand about 280px tall, from about 310px to 590px; nothing touches or overlaps them. The four corners carry everything else on 18px margins. Top-left: one supporting sentence at 18px on two lines, max 36ch, and 16px below it the single action as a solid ink rectangle. Top-right: a menu word or three text links at 15px. Bottom-left, on a baseline 24px above the edge: the city or the kind of work at 16px. Bottom-right on the same baseline: two or three text links at 15px. The ground between the corners and the name, about 230px above and below it, stays empty and is closed on both sides by the corner texts. The name returns in the footer at the same width with ground and ink swapped and a two-line invitation above it, so the page closes like a bracket.

On a phone: The name stays on one line across the width inside 16px margins and moves up to 22% of the height. The supporting sentence follows on the left margin at 17px, then the action at full width. The lower half holds the first picture of the work as a 4:5 frame cropped by the bottom edge, so the screen does not end in empty paper. A menu word sits top-right.
Headline: The name itself on one line, up to about 12 characters; the 18px sentence in the top-left corner says what it is.

Adjusted for this page:
- It names a kind of typeface: use the dealt faces (display Sentient, text Sentient) and their settings; capitals only where the type system sets them.

## 7. Sections, in order
Each is a composition to rebuild with this project's content. Keep the geometry; change the subject. Re-order or drop a
section when the argument of the page needs it (see references/copy.md), never to fall back to a card grid.

### 1. work — Book-spine index (index)
The complete index in the footprint of a banner. Twenty to forty project names are set as single lines rotated to read top to bottom with `writing-mode: vertical-rl`, packed side by side like book spines across the full content width. Each spine is a column 30–34px wide and 420px tall: the name at 18px starts at the top, and the discipline follows after a 12px gap on a flat highlighter-colour background chosen from four palette colours by discipline. Spines are separated only by 6px of space. Hovering or focusing a spine turns it to full ink and dims the others to 40%; the whole spine is the link. A two- or three-word heading at 28px sits top-left above the shelf with a one-sentence note at 15px to its right on the same baseline. No images. Below the shelf, 96px of empty ground. It turns a long client list into one dense typographic object.
On a phone: Rotation is dropped. Names become a horizontal wrap: each name at 17px followed by its highlighted discipline, items flowing inline with 10px gaps and 14px of line spacing, so the index reads as one dense paragraph of links filling the width.

Adjusted for this page:
- It fades text: anything a visitor reads, inactive items of a list included, is `--ink` or `--muted` at full strength (contrast 4.5:1 or more). The active item is told apart by `--ink` against `--muted`, weight or the accent, not by fading the rest.

### 2. list — One item per poster line (statement)
A short list set as a poster. Four to seven items (services, headline acts, courses, opening days) stack one per line at clamp(64px, 10vw, 150px) in the display weight, all capitals or all lowercase, leading 0.92, flush left at a 20px margin on a flat field. Its one detail (a price, a date, a time range) sits at the right margin on the same baseline at 18–20px with tabular figures, so the page has two alignments only: giant words left, small facts right. Items too long for one line at this size are shortened, not shrunk. Between items there is only the tight leading. Hovering an item swaps its ink for the accent. There is no heading: the first item starts 80px below the section top and the last ends 80px above the bottom. The list reuses the hero title's exact type size, so it reads as another poster.
On a phone: Items at 15vw, one per line, flush left with 16px margins. Each detail moves beneath its item at 15px, left-aligned, with 6px above and 22px below. If any single word is wider than the screen at 15vw, drop the whole list to 12vw rather than breaking a word.

Adjusted for this page:
- It names a kind of typeface: use the dealt faces (display Sentient, text Sentient) and their settings; capitals only where the type system sets them.

### 3. about — Side facts, wide paragraph (table)
Two rows share one vertical line at 34% of the viewport width. Row one: in a 160px column at the left margin, three label and value pairs stack with 20px between them, each a label in the soft ink with its value beneath in full ink, all at 13px (founded, based in, team size). From the 34% line a five-line paragraph at clamp(20px, 1.7vw, 26px), leading 1.35, runs to 90% of the width. Row two starts 120px lower and repeats the logic for the record of the practice: one plain word at 13px at the far left (awards, press, clients), then from the 34% line two list columns, each 260px wide with a 64px gutter, at 13px with 10px between lines: name at the left, year at the right in the soft ink. No heading; the paragraph opens the section. Each list comfortably holds 6–12 lines. No photographs are needed.
On a phone: Everything aligns to the left margin. The paragraph comes first at 19px. The three label and value pairs follow as a two-column grid at 14px. The two record lists stack with 40px between them, each opened by its plain word in full ink.

### 4. contact — One field, nothing else (stack)
The whole section is one prompt and one field in a column 520px wide, on the page's text column line or centred, with 200px of empty ground above and 160px below. The prompt is two lines at one size, 24–28px in medium weight: the first line in the soft ink, the second in full ink. 28px below, a single input 520px wide and 64px tall with a 12px radius, filled with the lightest tone on the page so it is the only bright surface in view; no outline at rest and a 2px inner ring in the accent on focus. Inside the right end of the field, 8px from its edge, a round submit button 44px across with an arrow and an accessible name. 16px below the field, one line at 13px says what happens next. 72px lower, three label and value rows at 14px: email, phone, address.
On a phone: The column fills the width with 20px margins. Prompt at 22px. The field stays one row, 60px tall, with the arrow button inside its right end. The three label and value rows stack with the label above each value and 20px between them.

Adjusted for this page:
- It names its own corners: use the one radius of this page (square unless the direction says otherwise) and one button shape.

## 8. Motion — Scaling wordmark
The giant name responds to the scroll: as the first screen leaves, the wordmark scales down a few percent (or grows from 0.94 to 1 as the footer arrives) on a scroll-linked timeline, so the monument feels heavy. Travel stays under 8%. Static fallback where scroll timelines are unsupported. Recipe: 'Scroll-linked progress' in references/motion.md, animating `scale`.
This is the signature move, the one loud moment. Around it the page still needs the rest of the motion layer (SKILL.md step 9): an entrance for the parts of every section, feedback on every control, and one moving picture. The inspector fails a page that barely moves. Recipes: references/motion.md.

## 10. Where the richness comes from
Scale contrast and colour mass. A capability list set at 5vw is an image in its own right.

## 11. How this direction goes wrong
Without pictures, weak copy has nowhere to hide: write real sentences. Vary the set pieces; four sections of big-heading-plus-paragraph is a text document, not a design. Keep all headings within two lines.

## Always
Headlines two lines or fewer at every width. No eyebrow labels, no monospace, no 01/02/03, no thin rules, no gradients, no shadows at rest,
no cream-and-terracotta, no violet. The frame: one h1 on the first screen that says what this is, equal outer margins, no
section huddled on one side with the other half empty, no blank stretch over half a screen, a heading on every section.
`inspect.mjs` measures all of it.
